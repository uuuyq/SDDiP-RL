A B S T R A C T
As a carbon-free storage medium, hydrogen has advantages in large-scale and long-term energy shifting,
thereby mitigating the seasonal imbalance between energy supply and demands. By integrating hydrogen,
electricity, heating and cooling, the hydrogen-enabled multi-energy microgrid (HMM) provides a desirable
test bed for decarbonizing the energy and power systems. In this paper, we study the networked HMMs
operation planning (NHOP) that optimizes the multi-timescale synergy of seasonal and short-term energy
storage. To hedge against the complex demand–supply uncertainties (e.g., seasonal fluctuation and hourly
variation of renewable power generation and energy demands), the NHOP problem is recast as a multistage
stochastic mixed-integer program (MS-MIP). Moreover, to overcome the computational challenges, a nested
decomposition algorithm based on stochastic dual dynamic integer programming (SDDiP) is tailored and
implemented. Case studies on a 33-bus test network with multiple HMMs demonstrate the economic benefits
of our operation planning strategy. The proposed NHOP model can well capture the seasonal and intra-day
dynamics of multi-type storage operation, which helps improve the cost-benefits under versatile practical
situations. Also, the customized SDDiP algorithm shows a strong scalable capacity for efficiently computing
large-scale MS-MIPs.
1. Introduction
Global climate change poses one of the greatest challenges to the
sustainable development of humanity. The target of controlling global
warming demands for the efforts of multiple social sectors to achieve
the carbon neutrality in the long-run [1]. The energy sectors, which
have contributed to nearly 40% CO2emissions [2], are particularly
urgent for a revolutionary transition to clean and low-carbon operation
patterns. Among the alternatives to carbon-intensive fossil fuels, hydrogen, which bears the merits of diverse sources, zero carbon emissions
and high energy density [3], has tremendous potential for a wide
range of applications in energy and power systems [4,5], transportation [6,7], and industrial sectors [8]. Notably, integrating hydrogen
with renewable energy sources (RESs), e.g., photovoltaic arrays (PVs)
and wind turbines (WTs), may contribute to fostering a sustainable energy future. In this way, renewable electricity can be effectively stored
by converting to hydrogen, enabling its subsequent on-site utilization

However, at the current stage, the hydrogen production through
electrolysis could be very costly [13]. It is critical to optimize the
operation of HMMs for enhancing the economic efficiency. To date,
the operational optimization of HMMs has been extensively studied in
many existing literature. Ref. [14] investigated the optimal operation of
hydrogen-based integrated systems, coordinating hydrogen, electricity,
cooling and heating. This study indicated that the synergy of hydrogen
and water storage has brought an 80% reduction in energy costs, while
achieving nearly 100% RES accommodation. Due to the low efficiency
of large-scale water electrolysis-based hydrogen production, an optimal
scheduling strategy for hydrogen-based microgrids was proposed in
Ref. [15], resulting in a 12.5% enhancement of ELZs’ utilization rates.
In Ref. [16], a market-oriented multi-timescale energy management
method for HMMs, comprising day-ahead and real-time economic dispatch, was studied, which could reduce the average diurnal operational
costs by 37.08%. Considering the distinguished preferences of different
operators, Ref. [17] proposed a multi-objective scheduling approach for
integrated power-hydrogen systems based on distributed 𝜉-constraint
optimization. This method was to optimize the main objective function
while ensuring the interests of other objective terms. To facilitate the
resilient operations against extreme events, e.g., natural disaster and
malicious attacks, Ref. [18] developed a risk-averse rolling-horizon
scheduling method for HMMs. As revealed by the numerical results, this
method could potentially delay the occurrence time of power supply
interruption, and improve the load survivability under disastrous occasions. Similarly, Ref. [19] presented a proactive scheduling method for
resilience-oriented operation of hydrogen systems. Moreover, considering the coordination of energy and transportation sectors, Ref. [20]
formulated an economical scheduling method for microgrids integrating hydrogen fueling stations, facilitating optimal on-site hydrogen
generation and refueling services for hydrogen vehicles (HVs).
We have noticed that, in these studies, the hydrogen storage is
usually utilized for intra-day or real-time energy regulation, which
demonstrates a similar functionality to ordinary storage types (e.g., batteries, supercapacitors or flywheel storage). Compared to conventional
storage facilities, however, the hydrogen storage is with a higher energy
density, which could be more suitable to perform the large-scale and
long-term energy shifting (e.g., the seasonal storage through underground salt caverns) [21]. Such technical advantage has significant
practical meanings for its application to handle the volatile and random
RES generation, which may follow an evident seasonal rhythm [22].
For instance, in Northern China, the peaks of solar generation generally
occur in Summer, while the wind power resources could be more
abundant during Spring and Winter months. On the other hand, the
seasonal pattern of multi-energy demands is also non-negligible [11].
For instance, cooling and heating demands could be concentratedly
consumed in Summer and Winter respectively, which happens to be
consistent with the peaking periods of power consumption. To keep
track with the seasonal fluctuation of energy supply and demands, we
believe that the seasonal hydrogen storage (SHS) needs to be properly
configured and optimally operated.
A few recent studies have concerned the planning and scheduling
of multi-energy systems equipping with SHS devices, e.g., in Refs. [21,
23–26]. Ref. [21] presented a mixed integer program (MIP) formulation to support the system design incorporating SHS. It indicated
that hydrogen storage operates in a seasonal pattern only when a
high storage capacity is available, which enabled smooth charging and
discharging. Ref. [23] showed that the SHS could further improve the
economical benefits of hydrogen-based integrated energy systems, by
addressing the randomness of RES outputs. Ref. [24] proposed a riskconstrained stochastic planning method for off-grid microgrids under
economics and resilience considerations. In this study, the levelized
system cost could be reduced by 0.0746 $/kWh through proper investment decisions on SHS and short-term energy storage. Ref. [25]
studied the energy management strategies of an off-grid microgrid
equipping with RESs, SHS and short-term battery storage. Through
the generated strategy, SHS could be fully exploited to maintain the
long-term energy balance, reducing the load shedding and renewable
energy curtailment. In Ref. [26], an optimal scheduling method based
on Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) was developed for an
integrated energy system with SHS, which improved the energy utilization efficiency, and mitigated the dependence on external energy
networks. In the existing publications, however, there lacks an in-depth
exploration on the complex and multi-timescale interaction among SHS
and various short-term storage devices. So it could be difficult to induce
a long-term economical operation strategy for networked HMMs to
realize the synergy of multiscale energy storage. Moreover, the current
uncertainty models are essentially static, which cannot capture the
dynamic storage scheduling decisions against the seasonal randomness
of RES generation and energy demands.
To address these challenges, this paper studied the long-term operational optimization of HMMs integrating RES units, SHS, short-term
energy storage (i.e., the daily hydrogen storage, battery banks and
thermal storage vessels) and H-CCHP systems. For the first time, the
networked HMMs operation planning (NHOP) problem is formulated
as a multistage program for characterizing the seasonal and diurnal
synergy of multiscale energy storage. Then, to handle the complex
supply–demand uncertainties, a stochastic extension of NHOP is presented. By leveraging the specific structure of scenario tree to discretely
represent the stochastic processes (including seasonal large-scale fluctuation and hourly small-scale variation of RES outputs as well as
the electrical, heating and cooling demands), we derive a multistage
stochastic mixed-integer program (MS-MIP) model. Moreover, based
on stochastic dual dynamic integer programming (SDDiP), a nested
decomposition algorithm is developed and customized to reduce the
computational burden for our stochastic formulation.
Compared to the current studies, the major contributions of this
paper can be summarized as three-fold:
1. A seasonal NHOP formulation is proposed, where the dynamic
interaction of short- and long-term energy storage, as well as
various energy conversion units are analytically modeled.
2. An MS-MIP extension for NHOP is developed to control the operational risks of HMMs associating with the multiscale supply–
demand uncertainties. Also, an SDDiP-based nested decomposition algorithm is designed and implemented to efficiently solve
this MS-MIP model.
3. Numerical studies on a 33-bus networked microgrids system
under real-world RES data demonstrate the economical benefits
of the proposed method. Through the coordinated scheduling
of SHS and short-term energy storage, a higher operational
flexibility can be achieved to reduce the risk cost.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2
proposes the NHOP formulation and its stochastic extension. Section 3
developed the nested decomposition algorithm based on SDDiP. Then,
we perform case studies in Section 4 to demonstrate the effectiveness
of our NHOP model and SDDiP algorithm. Finally, conclusions are
attained and discussed in Section 5.

heating demands. The whole procedure mentioned above should be

4.1. Analysis of long-term operation results
We first present the long-term operation scheme of HMMs under an
average scenario to investigate the synergy of multi-type energy storage
devices. Figs. 5–7 show the NHOP results of electrical, hydrogen,
as well as heating and cooling subsystems, respectively. Each figure
contains two or four typical days corresponding to different seasons,
which captures the seasonal and diurnal patterns of system operation.
Our results are elaborated and discussed as in the following:
• Electrical Subsystem: According to Fig. 5, the operational decisions
of electrical subsystem are made based on the varying patterns
of RES outputs, electrical demands, and TOU electricity prices.
As shown in Fig. 5(a), because the wind power resources are
obviously more abundant in Spring than the other seasons while
the demands level is relatively lower, the electrical demands in
a typical Spring day can be mostly fulfilled by RES outputs.
Moreover, during 8:00–18:00, since the summation of WT and
PV generation exceeds the load demand, both the BSs and ELZs
are activated to store the surplus energy. For such a Spring
scenario, the regional electrical subsystem can autonomously operate without the support of utility power grid. The operation
strategies in Autumn day (as in Fig. 5(c)) are similar to the Spring,
except the needs for grid power imports and the less frequent
utilization of energy storage devices. That is because the WT
outputs are much lower along with a higher electrical demands
level in Autumn. In comparison, the situations in Summer and
Winter could be quite different, associating with the variation
of heating and cooling demands. As exhibited in Fig. 5(b), for
a typical Summer day, our optimization results suggest a mixed
power supply scheme based on the generation of WTs, PVs and
FCs. It mainly relies on utility power procurement during early
morning (e.g., 1:00–7:00) to energize the electrolyzers under the
valley of TOU prices, in preparation for satisfying the power and
cooling demands in subsequent periods. The power import also
occurs in night time (e.g., 20:00–24:00) when PVs’ generation
is unavailable. Similarly, in Winter (see Fig. 5(d)), it intends to
purchase power from the utility grid in the morning time but with
a smaller amount, because the WTs’ generation is more abundant
than Summer. Besides, the FCs operate under a relatively high
setting point during 5:00–10:00 following the peak periods of
heating demands.

storage facilities, which is with a smaller unit cost than the curtailment

configuration of multi-type energy storage.

5. Conclusion and discussions
In this paper, we propose an MS-MIP method to support the networked HMMs operation planning considering seasonal and short-term
energy storage. To relieve the computational burden of computing
large-scale MS-MIP, a nested decomposition algorithm based on SDDiP
is customized and developed. Case studies on a 33-bus integrated energy network verify the effectiveness of the proposed NHOP model and
SDDiP algorithm. Some key observations and insights are summarized
as follows:
• The seasonal hydrogen storage provides a long-term and large-scale
energy regulation capability. Our results suggest that the SHS
charges in transitions seasons (Spring and Autumn) for fully utilizing the RES generation, while prepares for the upcoming demands
of centralized cooling and heating consumption in Summer and
Winter. The coordination of SHS with short-term energy storage
helps achieve a higher operational flexibility for mitigating the
demand–supply mismatches in a district multi-energy network.
• The proposed operation planning method offers an analytical tool to
generate economical operation strategies for networked HMMs.
Moreover, it can provide an informative planning guidance for the
capacity configuration of RES units and multi-type energy storage. Compared to conventional storage equipment, SHS could be
with a more flexible design space for enhancing the system-level
economic benefits.
• The multi-stage stochastic formulation could help the grid operators to flexibly adjust their operation strategies against versatile
practical situations, which effectively decreases the risk cost. Also,
the customized SDDiP algorithm provides a powerful and scalable
computational tool to deal with large-scale MS-MIPs. Compared
to a state-of-the-art commercial solver (i.e., Gurobi), our method
has reduced the solution time by more than 94% when ≥125
