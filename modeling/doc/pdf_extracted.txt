A Hierarchical Framework-Based Coordinated
Optimization of Building HVAC Systems and EVs
Haoming Zhao , Student Member, IEEE, Zhanbo Xu , Member, IEEE, Jiang Wu , Member, IEEE,
Fengxia Liu, Student Member, IEEE, and Xiaohong Guan , Life Fellow, IEEE
Abstract— The demands of electric vehicles (EVs) and building heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems
have considerable flexibility. Their flexibility is influenced by
occupants’ behavior resulting in huge complementary and dispatchable capability. Therefore, the coordination of EVs and
HVAC systems holds significant potential for optimizing the building demand profiles and energy cost under time-of-use (TOU)
tariffs. However, solving the coordinated problem in practice
still faces the challenges in computational complexity and global
information requirement due to the spatio-temperal coupling
constraints. In this paper, a mixed-integer linear programming
model is developed to formulate the multi-building energy system
with EVs and the impact of occupants’ behavior on the demand
and flexibility of the system. The problem is converted into
a three-level structure using Lagrangian relaxation framework.
A dynamic programming-based Lagrangian relaxation (DPLR)
algorithm is developed to independently solve all sub-problems of
the three-level structure in a decomposition and coordination way
while avoiding the iterative computation between the middle and
lower level. The numerical results show the developed method can
obtain a near-optimal solution in an efficient way without perceivable degradation in accuracy, which is 4% worse but at least
three times faster, compared to the existing centralized algorithm.
Note to Practitioners—The escalating demand for EVs and
HVAC systems results in increased energy costs and challenges to
existing power systems, such as frequency deviations and higher
peak loads. Therefore, this paper focuses on the coordinated
optimization of the EV charging and building HVAC system
operation, while considering rooftop photovoltaic generation
supply within the system. Optimal coordination of the above
system can effectively reduce energy costs under TOU tariffs
and enhance the ability to utilize renewable energy sources.
However, solving the coordinated optimization problem still faces
difficulties since the computational complexity will exponentially
grow with increasing problem scale due to the spatio-temperal
coupling between the demand of EVs and HVAC systems.

I. INTRODUCTION
A
FTER the remarkable development of electric vehicle
(EV) over the past decade, the total number of EVs have
been raising to 26 million in 2022 [1]. However, the increasing
number of EVs has resulted in a significant increase in electricity consumption and brought challenges to power grids [2],
such as frequency deviation and higher peak loads [3], [4].
On the other hand, building energy systems (BES) account for
over 40% of energy consumption in the U.S., among them,
heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems
consume 45% of the electricity energy [5]. Due to room indoor
temperature dynamics and EVs’ required charging time being
much shorter than their parking duration, the coordination of
EVs and HVAC systems provides huge potential in enhancing
the flexibility of BES for demand side management [6].
To coordinately optimize the operation of EVs and BES,
various studies have been made to minimize their total
cost [7], [8], improve the utilization efficiency of distributed
renewable energy sources [9], reduce peak load and reshape
demand profiles [10]. EVs and buildings typically share the
same infrastructure, Contreras-Ocana et al. [11] proposed
a coordinated optimization problem of EVs and buildings
considering the demand limits, peak demand charges and
itemized billing tariffs to optimize their demand flexibility.
Wang et al. [12] developed a three-level scheduling model
for aggregated HVAC systems and EVs under the locational
marginal price in the competitive market to make benefit for
both distribution systems and aggregators.
The coordinated problem of EVs and HVAC systems is
usually large-scale. Many efforts have been made to tackle the
curse of dimensionality. E.g., Hurtado et al. [13] developed
a multi-agent based decentralized approach to optimize the
EVs charging strategy and reshape the demand profile of BES.
Yang et al. [14] developed a decentralized EV-based charging
algorithm under the Lagrangian relaxation (LR) framework
to determine multiple EVs charging based on the building’s
electrical and thermal demand. In order to improve the computational efficiency by reducing the state space, the event-based
approaches are also developed to solve the optimization problem of EVs and building HVAC systems, since the state space
aggregation can be realized by defining several events [15],
[16], [17]. Furthermore, other decentralized algorithms, such
as Dantzig-Wolfe decomposition [18], Bender’s decomposition [19], and the alternating direction method of multipliers
algorithm (ADMM) [20] are also developed to solve the
coordinated problem. Although many innovative results in the
coordinated optimization of EVs and building HVAC systems
are reported in the literature, the computational efficiency and
convergence of existing methods are still hard to be guaranteed
due to the spatio-temporal coupling constraints caused by
occupant’s thermal comfort requirement and mobility.
Thus, there are still many challenges in the coordination
of EVs and building HVAC systems. First, spatio-temporal
coupling. Multiple buildings are coupled in space due to
the mobility of EVs. In addition, the operation of EVs and
HVAC are also coupled in time due to the thermal comfort and next trip requirement of occupants. So, the above
spatio-temporal coupling between multiple buildings and EVs
needs to be well formulated to maximally enhance the flexibility provided by EVs and HVAC systems. Second, multiple
stakeholders. Multiple stakeholders of EVs and buildings are
involved in the energy conversion and interaction process of
the coordination of EVs and HVAC systems. The centralized
algorithm is thus limited since the global information is costly
to collect in practice due to occupants’ privacy concerns
and high communication and computation costs. Third, the
curse of dimensionality. The state space and policy space
of the coordinated problem will exponentially grow with
the increasing scale of EVs and buildings. The coordinated
problem is believed to be NP-hard [14], [15]. In addition,
due to the spatio-temporal coupling, the existing decentralized
method cannot be directly applied since their optimality and
convergence may not be guaranteed. Therefore, an effective
and efficient decentralized optimization method needs to be
developed based on the structure of the coordinated problem
for practical engineering.
In order to overcome the challenges mentioned above,
we make the following contributions in this paper. First,

spatio-temporal coupling and interaction between EVs and

analytic solution of EV charging sub-problems in the lower
level are derived by dynamic programming. The dual problems
of EV charging facility operation sub-problem in the middle
level are transformed from “max-min” structure problems to
“max” optimization problems of the Lagrangian multipliers,
thus avoiding the inner iteration between the middle and lower
level. In this way, the sub-problems of each HVAC system
operation and the sub-problems of each EV charging can be
independently solved. Numerical results show the coordinated
optimization can get better utilization of the complementary
flexibility of EVs and HVAC systems demand and save over
44% of the energy cost. The performance of the DPLR
algorithm developed in this paper has great improvement
that at least three times faster than the centralized algorithm
with less than 4% worse accuracy. It also can save 52%
computational time with better accuracy compared with the
traditional LR algorithm.

In Section II, the coordinated problem is formulated as a
centralized MILP problem. In Section III, the decentralized
problem of a three-level structure is developed based on
the LR framework and the DPLR algorithm is developed.
In Section IV, the case studies and numerical results are presented to validate the performance of the DPLR algorithm. The
discussion of the performance of developed DPLR algorithm is
given in Section V. A brief conclusion is given in Section VI.
II. PROBLEM FORMULATION
This paper focuses on the coordination of multi-building
energy system with EVs as shown in Fig. 1. There may be
various kinds of buildings, e.g., residential buildings, office
buildings and commercial buildings in the system. Within
each building, there are amount of rooms with HVAC system, parking lots with charging facility, roof-top photovoltaic
generation devices and other electrical equipments. There
are bidirectional energy interactions between EVs and each
building, including EV charging from buildings and feeding
energy into buildings through charging facility in buildings.
Fig. 1. Multi-building energy system with EVs.
As similar to [21] and [22], we assume that there is a central
coordinator among buildings in the system (e.g., the aggregator
of buildings or the scheduling center of the city or town).
It is connected with all buildings to collect global information
(such as the trip plans and initial state of charge of EVs,
and the power supplied by roof-top photovoltaic generation)
and broadcasts centralized coordination information to help
all buildings make decision for their operation of HVAC
systems and EVs charging, while satisfying the occupant
thermal comfort requirement and the trip energy requirement
of occupant mobility in daily life.
In this section, the coordinated optimization problem of the
multi-building energy system with EVs is formulated as a
discrete-time-based MILP problem. The objective is to minimize the energy cost of the multi-building energy system under
TOU tariff mechanism while satisfying the occupant’s thermal
comfort requirement and mobility requirement. In the following, the objective function is given in sub-section III-A. The
operation constraints including the coupling between occupant
thermal comfort and mobility requirement are developed in
sub-section III-B. The centralized coordinated optimization
problem is described in sub-sections III.C.

requirement. The EVs can only charging and discharging in
the buildings. So, the energy cost of EVs is considered in the

scheduling horizon is defined as

the increasing scale of the problem. Solving such optimization problem faces the computational complexity challenge.
Furthermore, the central coordinator has to collect all the
global information for the purpose of solving the centralized
problem. In practice, collecting global information may face
difficulties in high transmission requirement and occupants’
privacy concerns. Therefore, A DPLR algorithm is developed
in Section III to let EVs and HVAC systems make their
decision in a decomposition and coordination way.
III. HIERARCHICAL FRAMEWORK-BASED OPTIMIZATION
ALGORITHM

engineering. In order to tackle the challenges, P1 is equivalently decomposed into a hierarchical framework with three
levels based on Lagrangian relaxation in sub-section III-A.
A DPLR algorithm is developed to solve the hierarchical
framework-based problem in subsection III-B.

information exchange mechanism between different levels is

B. Occupant’s Information Privacy Protection
In the centralized algorithm, the central coordinator has to
collect all the global information (including the trip plans
and initial state of charge of EVs, and the power supplied
by roof-top photovoltaic generation) from the occupants to
make optimal decision and scheduling the demand of EVs and
HVAC systems to satisfy occupant’s requirement and reduce
the energy cost under TOU tariffs.
In practice, occupants may not be willing to share all the
information to central coordinator due to privacy concern.
Thus, the DPLR algorithm is developed in this paper. In this
way, the occupants of EVs and HVAC systems can make their
own decision in a decomposition and coordination way according to the Lagrangian multipliers. Instead of uploading the
private information such as trip plan and get the optimal action
signal from the centralized way, based on DPLR algorithm, the
central coordinator only needs to collect the information of
the aggregated demand of EVs and HVAC systems uploaded
by occupants to calculate the optimal Lagrangian multipliers.
The trip plan and demand information of single occupant is
not necessary for coordinately scheduling. It can partly protect
the occupant’s privacy.
C. Information Exchange Mechanism
In DPLR algorithm, the central coordinator broadcasts the
Lagrangian multipliers λ to EV charging facility and rooms
in buildings. EV charging facility spreads the Lagrangian
multipliers µ to EVs. The EVs upload their demand
information to EV charging facility and buildings collect and
upload the aggregated demand information of EV charging
facility and rooms to central coordinator to calculate the
optimal Lagrangian multipliers µ and λ. By introducing
the Lagrangian multipliers µ and λ, the sub-problems of
HVAC systems and the sub-problems of EVs are decoupled
due to the relaxation of the power balance constraints.
In this way, EVs and HVAC systems can make their own
decision individually in a decomposition and coordination
way according to the Lagrangian multipliers λ and µ which
are treated as price signal.
VI. CONCLUSION
A coordinated optimization problem of the multi-building
energy system with EVs is investigated in this paper. The
problem is formulated as a centralized optimization problem to
capture the spatio-temporal coupling and interaction between
EVs and HVAC systems caused by occupant’s thermal comfort
requirement and mobility. The problem is converted into
a three-level optimization problem base on LR framework
in a decomposition and coordination way to overcome the
computational complexity and global information requirement
challenge. However, the convergence and accuracy of solving
the optimization problem with the three-level structure is hard
to be guaranteed due to the double-layer iterations. A DPLR
algorithm is developed to transform the dual problems of
EV charging facility operation sub-problem from “max-min”
structure problem to “max” optimization problems in order
to solve it directly. The numerical result shows that the
coordination of EVs and building HVAC system can reduce
44% of the energy cost by utilizing their load flexibility. The
developed DPLR algorithm is at least three times faster than
the centralized algorithm with less than 4% worse accuracy.
It also can save 52% with better accuracy compared with the
traditional LR algorithm.
